About

Clinical training, a first publication, and a change of direction toward the brain.

I am a sixth-year medical student at Petre Shotadze Tbilisi Medical Academy in Georgia. My first publication was a microbiology study of antibiotic resistance patterns in Staphylococcus aureus, where I did the laboratory work, the statistics and the data visualization.

That taught me how to run a study from bench to manuscript. My interest has since moved to computational and systems neuroscience, and to neuropsychiatry in particular: how signals from the brain, such as EEG and imaging, can support more objective diagnosis of psychiatric conditions.

The research radar and the lab on the site are built to be useful, not decorative: the radar tracks new work from Swiss labs as it is published, and the lab explains models I study, in English and in German, for colleagues in the German-speaking research world too.
